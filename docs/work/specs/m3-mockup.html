Locations — the first time a director sees “places”

One coherent experience in three parts: a Locations setup screen, a place picker inside an activity, and the first-run reconciliation of what the upgrade inferred from the old free-text data. Real Shoresh colors, type, and motion. Locations are always optional — a camp that only wants named rooms is never taxed, and nothing here makes a map mandatory.

The place picker, inside an activity

replaces the free-text Location input
live — type to try it

This is where most directors actually touch locations. Setting up “Archery”, they pick a place — or type one that doesn’t exist and create it right there, without leaving the activity. Try typing be, or a brand-new name like Lake.

The three states, spelled out below (the live one above cycles through them):
a · typeahead over existing places
b · a name that doesn’t exist yet
Creates the place (1 group by default) and binds it. It shows up on the Locations screen later — no trip to go administer a list.
c · selected
Pool· 3 groups at once
The capacity meta reminds the director what the schedule will hold this activity to.
d · cleared / no place (valid — optional)
Leaving it blank is fine. Not every activity has a room.

Effortless is the requirement. Typeahead filters as they type; the create row is the last option, set off by a hairline in forest-green so “this makes something new” reads instantly. Keyboard: ↑/↓ to move, Enter to pick, Esc to close. Popover uses the shared popFade (scale .97→1 + fade, 180ms).

3

First-run reconciliation · the un-missable merge gate

hard requirement — ADR + Red Hat
first open after upgrade

The very first time Locations opens after the upgrade, Shoresh reconciles what it inferred from the old free-text data. Two places spelled differently (Pool / pool) are two separate rooms to the engine, silently splitting a capacity the director now trusts. So the merge is a gate — a blocking first-run step, not a banner you can scroll past.

Before you start 1 of 1

These look like the same place

Your old schedule used “Pool” and “pool”. Shoresh kept them separate so it wouldn’t change your data without asking — but that splits how many groups fit. Merge them into one place, or say they’re genuinely different.

Keep the spelling you want:
Pool3 groups at once
4 activities here
pool1 group at once
2 activities here
Room for
groups at once after merging.
You can undo this. The merged place stays in Trash if you change your mind.

Impossible to miss, still reversible, director in control. A blocking overlay (not a dismissible chrome banner) with the deep-navy scrim from DESIGN_STANDARD. It steps one pair at a time (“1 of 1” here). “Merge” re-points both rooms’ activities to the surviving spelling and keeps the roomier capacity; “different places” is an explicit decision, not a wave-away. Both clear the gate. Everything is undoable (Trash). Motion: card Fade+Settle in; on decide, it settles closed and the screen behind is revealed.
