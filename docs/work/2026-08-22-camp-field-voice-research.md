Reference research to move Shoresh's UI voice from generic-warm to **camp-director / camp-field
specific**, using the real vernacular of the Jewish camp world. Primary-sourced; informs the W7b
voice pass. All tone/vernacular claims cited; rewrite wordings are synthesis (proposals).

## Headline finding (drives everything)
**Foundation for Jewish Camp's own admin/HR copy uses plain English; Hebrew is reserved for the
experiential/program layer, not the management layer.** So Shoresh's *chrome* should be camp-field
**English** (groups, divisions, electives, rotation, specialists, unit head, staff, the day) — this
alone reads field-native without a single Hebrew word. Hebrew belongs glossed/opt-in, in program
contexts.

## The register to hit
Warm, relational, mission-framed — sounds like it was **built by a camp person**, plain-spoken
enough for a director who knows zero Hebrew. FJC frames directors as "meaning-makers, not just
administrators"; the field's voice is "become stronger, together," never "optimize your workflow."

## Voice guideline (10 principles, condensed)
1. Sound like a fellow camp person, not a SaaS. Warmth + confidence, few exclamation points.
2. Default to the **operational nouns of camp, in English**: groups, divisions, activities, electives, rotation, the day, free play, specialists, staff, counselors, unit head.
3. Prefer camp-field English over software English: "rotation/the day/free play/session/specialist/division" over "item/entry/record/resource/slot/configuration."
4. Use Hebrew **only where the field does, and always glossed on first use** — `chugim (electives)`, `edah (division)`, `t'fillah (prayer)`. Never Hebrew-only on a load-bearing control.
5. Make Hebrew **opt-in, not imposed** (the safe answer to "how much Hebrew"). Default English chrome; echo back Hebrew the director themselves typed; optional vocabulary setting.
6. **Match vocabulary to camp type.** Do NOT hardcode overnight-only Hebrew (tzrif/bunk, menucha, nikayon) — day camps don't use them. Divisions/groups/electives/activities are the common denominator.
7. Frame the director as running something joyful and meaningful, not doing data entry — a little of that spirit in empty states/celebrations, then get out of the way.
8. Reserve warmth for moments; working screens stay quiet (matches the existing art placement rule).
9. Errors: plain, honest, camp-calm — never cutesy about failure.
10. When in doubt, say the plain English thing a director would say out loud ("groups", "the schedule", "electives", "free swim", "specialists").

## OWNER DECISION (open, brand call): how much Hebrew by default?
- **(a) English-first, Hebrew opt-in** — recommended; matches FJC's own admin practice, safest for mixed Hebrew-literacy + day camps.
- (b) Bilingual chrome (Hebrew + gloss everywhere) — warmer/immersive, heavier, skews overnight.
- (c) Director-authored only (English chrome; Hebrew only where the director typed it).
Recommendation: (a) + (c) together.
